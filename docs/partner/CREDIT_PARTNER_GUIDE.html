Credit Partner Guide

How the young people already in your program can earn high school credit for taking part, what Optio handles, and what to tell their families.

What this is

Optio Academy is an accredited private high school. Students earn credit by documenting real work rather than by sitting in a classroom for a set number of hours.

That means what you already run can count as a high school class. Your participants enroll in Optio Academy, take part in your program exactly as they do now, and finish with credit on an official transcript that Optio issues and sends to their school.

You do not become a school. You do not grade anyone. Nothing about how you run your program has to change.

What your participants get

  • [0.5] credit in [Subject] on an official high school transcript, issued by an accredited school.
  • Credit that transfers the same way credit from any other school does.
  • A record of what they actually did. Photos, video, and notes gathered as they go, kept in one place they own.
  • No homework, no tests, no extra sessions. Taking part in your program is the coursework.

How it works

  1. Optio sets up your program

    One conversation. We agree on the subject, how much credit a term is worth, your dates, and what counts as taking part. You never touch a settings page.

  2. You share one link

    Families enroll themselves. The account, the parent consent, and the school their transcript should go to are all collected in that one form. You do not gather, hold, or forward any of it.

  3. Participants take part

    Same practices, same lessons, same season. Whether a young person is earning credit is invisible in the room.

  4. They document as they go

    In the Optio app, a participant adds photos, a short video, or a few sentences about what they worked on. It takes a minute and it is theirs to keep.

  5. You confirm who took part

    At the end of the term you open your roster in Optio, check the names of the people who genuinely participated, and submit. This is the only administrative task the program asks of you, and it takes about two minutes.

  6. Optio reviews and awards the credit

    A licensed Optio teacher reviews the work and awards the credit. Optio issues the transcript and sends it to the school each family named when they enrolled.

Your part, precisely

Optio asks you to

  • Share the enrollment link with your families.
  • Answer questions about your own program.
  • Confirm at the end of the term who took part.
  • Tell us if someone stops attending partway through.

Optio never asks you to

  • Grade anyone, write lesson plans, or change your curriculum.
  • Collect, store, or forward student records.
  • Award the credit yourself.

Questions families will ask you

Will our school accept it?

Most do. The credit comes from an accredited private school on an official transcript with a verification link, which is the same form a registrar handles for any transfer student. A district can still set its own transfer policy, so a family who is counting on the credit should ask their counselor before enrolling. Optio will talk to the counselor directly if that helps.

Does my child have to do extra work?

No. Showing up and taking part is the work. Documenting it in the app takes a minute here and there, and it is the part most participants end up enjoying, because they finish the season with something to show for it.

What if we stop partway through?

Then no credit is awarded for that term and nothing else happens. There is no penalty and no mark on any record.

Who can see my child's work?

The student, their parent, and the Optio teacher who reviews it. Nothing is public unless the family deliberately makes it public. Optio does not sell data and does not put students in advertising tools.

Is this a real high school transcript?

Yes. It is issued by Optio Academy, which is accredited by the Accrediting Commission for Schools, Western Association of Schools and Colleges, the same commission that accredits thousands of public and private schools.

How to talk about the accreditation

The accreditation is what makes this worth anything, so there are a few firm rules about how it is described. They are short, and Optio will help you get any wording right.

  • Say the credit comes from Optio Academy. Participants earn credit through Optio Academy, an accredited private high school. That is accurate and it is the strongest thing you can say.
  • Do not describe your own program as accredited. It is not, and it does not need to be. Optio Academy holds the accreditation and issues the credit.
  • Do not use the commission's logo. Optio is licensed to display it. Partners are not.
  • Wherever you mention accreditation in writing, include the commission's full details in the same page or email. The block at the bottom of this guide is exactly what to paste.
  • Send new wording to Optio before it goes live. A quick read takes a day and saves everyone a correction later.

The shortest compliant version: "Credit is awarded by Optio Academy, accredited by the Accrediting Commission for Schools, Western Association of Schools and Colleges, 533 Airport Blvd., Suite 200, Burlingame, CA 94010, www.acswasc.org."
